Overview Accept Stake Pool How it works

Somebody takes 2.9% of every card payment. It doesn't have to be a company.

A processor charges a fee to run a network. That part is fair. What isn't obvious is why the fee should become profit for a corporation rather than going back to the people who generated it.

Here it doesn't. Every fee goes to stakers in the transaction it was collected — and the token they stake started at zero supply, with every unit minted by a merchant processing a payment. Nobody was allocated any, including whoever deployed it.

One payment, start to finish

1
A customer pays 100.00 USDC One signature. The amount shown is the amount charged — the fee comes out of it, not on top.
2
The merchant receives 97.50 In that same transaction. Not tonight, not in two days — the money never stops moving long enough to be held by anyone.
3
2.50 lands in the staking pool Every staker's claim rises the instant it arrives. Nobody decides later what to do with it, because there is no function that could.
4
The merchant is minted 100 SWIPE One per dollar processed. This is the only way supply is ever created — there is no premine, no treasury, and no issuance function for anyone to call.
5
Stakers are emitted 2.50 esSWIPE Alongside the USDC, at a fixed ratio to the fee. Emissions track payments rather than a clock, so a quiet month emits nothing and there is no schedule to front-run.

The bigger difference is when. Card settlement is two or three days, and during those days your money is earning interest for somebody else. Here it is in the merchant's wallet before the customer has put their phone away.

Stake

Why it settles instantly

Card money moves through an acquirer, a network, and an issuer before it reaches you, and each of them wants a day. That's where the two-to-three-day wait comes from — not from anything technical, but from a chain of intermediaries each holding your money briefly on the way past.

Here there's no chain. The customer signs once and the split happens inside that transaction: your share to your wallet, the fee to the pool. Nothing is held between transactions, because there's no code path that would hold it — the contract has no balance to sweep and no owner to sweep it.

And no chargebacks

A settled payment is settled. That's a genuine advantage for anyone who's eaten a fraudulent dispute sixty days after doing the work, and a genuine disadvantage for the customer, who has no recourse if you don't deliver.

Which side that suits depends entirely on your business. It suits contractors invoicing commercial customers. It suits a stranger buying something online far less. The guide covers what this does and doesn't do in more detail than a front page should.
